The problem

Brachytherapy needs real-time guidance

Brachytherapy is a cornerstone of curative treatment for locally advanced cervical cancer. Its success depends on seeing the tumor and the organs around it clearly.

Today

The best imaging is the hardest to reach

MRI and CT are the preferred guides for brachytherapy, but they come with real costs for clinics and patients.

  • Costly and scarceMRI and CT scanners are expensive and limited, especially in low-resource settings where cervical cancer is most common.
  • Multiple roomsMoving between procedure and imaging rooms slows the workflow and lengthens a painful procedure.
  • Patients lost along the wayLonger, harder procedures increase the risk that patients do not complete treatment.
The opportunity

Ultrasound already works, but it is hard to use

Ultrasound-guided brachytherapy has been shown to be as effective as MRI- or CT-guided treatment, and it is the standard at leading cancer centers. Yet it remains underused.

AffordableA fraction of the cost of MRI or CT.
PortableBrought to the patient, in the treatment room.
Widely availableAlready present in clinics around the world.

What holds ultrasound back

  • Unfamiliar anatomyMany radiation oncologists have limited experience reading pelvic ultrasound in real time.
  • Variability between operatorsResults depend heavily on who is holding the probe and reading the image.
  • Manual measurementStructures are measured and outlined by hand, which takes time.
  • Device placementPositioning the tandem and interstitial needles without clear guidance can be suboptimal.
  • No 3D data for doseWithout a 3D reconstruction, dose to the cervix and nearby organs is hard to calculate accurately.
Our approach

A single-room, AI-guided workflow

Mirthena's machine learning software automatically identifies anatomy and devices on ultrasound, giving clinicians clear, consistent guidance from imaging to plan.

  1. Ultrasound

    The patient is imaged with ultrasound in the procedure room.

  2. AI identifies anatomy

    Structures are labelled and the cervix and organs at risk are outlined automatically.

  3. AI guides devices

    The tandem and interstitial needles are located, with guidance on positioning.

  4. Physician decides

    The physician reviews, adjusts and approves a 3D plan with dose calculation.

Why it matters

Cervical cancer weighs heaviest where advanced imaging is scarce. Making ultrasound easier to use can shorten procedures, reduce variability and extend high-quality, image-guided brachytherapy to more patients, in well-resourced and low-resource clinics alike.
